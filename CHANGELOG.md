# Changelog

## Unreleased

## 0.5.18 — 2026-10-25

- **Backpanel wire routing and zip-tie bundles (`panelcad.core.panel_wiring`)** — Routes schematic From-To conductors across the physical enclosure layout through connected wire trays (`wt-top`, `wt-mid`, `wt-bot`, `wt-left`, `wt-right`) and interactive **Zip-Tie Waypoints** (`ZipTieWaypoint`: slice across wires to bundle, drag to adjust, or remove to unbundle).
- **Nearest-tray vertical entry and strict in-tray pathing (`verify_routes_inside_trays`)** — Conductors enter the nearest horizontal wire tray directly from each device terminal, stay strictly inside the wire-tray network in parallel lanes, and turn at slot entries and tray intersections with compact smooth radii (`R <= 4.0 mm`, zero 90° corners).
- **Wire-by-Wire cut-length schedule (`WireByWireSchedule`)** — Calculates per-wire shop cut lengths (2D routed arc length + source/destination terminal Z-drops + duct depth allowance + strip/termination slack, rounded up to `0.5"` / `10 mm`), exports CSV schedules, and generates a printable **Wire-by-Wire Cut Sheet** (`kind="wire_by_wire"`).
- **Technician Wiring Walkthrough mode (`WiringWalkthroughPanel`)** — Steps through one wire route at a time (`Step k of N`) on the normal backpanel layout view (`Reports → Wiring Walkthrough / Cut Sheet…`) with `FROM:` / `TO:` terminal callouts, directional flow chevrons, installation checklist state, and shop cut length banner.

## 0.5.17 — 2026-10-25

- **Picture-to-Print and PDF/scan round-trip** — Fixed `offpage_from_match` neighbor-pin resolution during vector and raster print import, restoring 100% round-trip connectivity and wire-number recall across exported PDFs and scans.
- **Phone photo preprocessing** — `panelcad.importer.preprocess` adds automatic 4-corner perspective rectification, uneven lighting and shadow normalization, and camera EXIF `72/96 DPI` page-scale normalization before symbol and wire extraction.
- **Universal From-To extraction and Clean NEMA Ladder synthesis** — `panelcad.importer.ladder_synth` (`infer_rungs`) extracts a universal From-To wire schedule from imported prints, surfaces an interactive **From-To Wire Schedule** tab in `ImportReviewPanel`, and synthesizes a **Clean NEMA Ladder** (`layout_mode="ladder"`) directly from extracted connectivity.
- **As-built reconstruction ladder and 3-phase power split** — `panelcad.reconstruct` builds a clean horizontal NEMA control ladder (`"asbuilt"`) and splits 3-phase motor and feeder circuits onto a dedicated vertical power feeder sheet (`"asbuilt_power"`) from traced wire landings.
- **3-phase power sheet rendering and checks** — Suppressed single-phase `L1`/`L2` ladder rails on 3-phase power sheets (`power_sheet_has_ladder_rails`), added `L1`/`L2`/`L3` 3-phase supply T-caps and source callouts (`missing_three_phase_supply_callouts`), and traced continuous 3-phase phase colors through power devices.

## 0.5.16 — 2026-10-24

- **IEC-to-NEMA From-To gate** — Standard conversion runs `verify_converted_from_to` (`check_connections`, scorer `from-to-check/1`) before and after conversion. If any net is lost, added, or merged, `ConnectionLost` rolls back the change and does not write the output file. Chunk reverts are guarded the same way with `RevertRefused`. Wire numbering supports NEMA sheet-and-line (`line_ref`) or preserving existing labels (`keep`), and IEC terminal blocks map `-X1:3` to `TB1-3`.
- **NEMA and IEC symbol pairs** — Added IEC and NEMA symbol pairs across pushbuttons, selector switches, E-stops, limit/pressure/float/temperature/proximity switches, pilot lights, horns, contactors, relays, on-delay and off-delay timers, fuses, breakers, thermal overload relays, disconnects, control transformers, 24 V power supplies, VFDs, soft starters, 3-phase motors, PLC digital and analog I/O modules, and terminal blocks (`panelcad.convert.standard_map`). NEMA symbols follow NEMA ICS 19, ANSI Y32.2, and IEEE 315 geometry with at least 1.5 mm border clearance, and single-pole control fuses and breakers stay on the control color rather than 3-phase power colors.
- **Role-based tags, pins, cross-references, and power sheets** — `ROLE_PREFIX_TABLE` (`ROLE_TAG_TABLE`) renames tags by circuit role (`S`/`SP`/`ST`/`SL`/`SQ`/`SB` to `PB`/`SEL`/`ES`/`LS`/`PRS`/`FS`/`TSW`/`PXS`/`SSW`, `K`/`KM`/`KA`/`KT`/`KS` to `M`/`CR`/`TR`/`SR`, `Q`/`QF`/`QS`/`QM` to `CB`/`DISC`/`MCP`, `F`/`FU`/`FR` to `FU`/`OL`, `T` to `T` with alias `XFMR`, `G` to `PS`, `U` to `VFD`/`SS` with alias `DRV`, `H`/`HL`/`HA` to `PL`/`HN` with alias `LT`, `A` to `PLC`, and `X`/`PE` to `TB`/`GND` with alias `X`) while contacts inherit their parent coil tag. Contact pins map IEC `13/14` to `3/4`, `11/12` and `21/22` to `1/2`, and overload `95/96` and `97/98` to NEMA OL contacts. Converted NEMA sheets get rung numbers and coil contact tables, 3-phase power sheets keep their vertical feeder layout without control-ladder relayout or right-rail collisions, and title-block and wire overlap guards keep rungs inside the drawing frame.
- **Multi-page IEC consolidation** — `consolidate_multipage_iec` (`consolidate_control_sheets`) merges multi-sheet IEC control circuits linked by off-page arrows onto horizontal NEMA ladder sheets and removes empty secondary control sheets while keeping every From-To net intact.
- **Conversion decisions and Problems review** — Ambiguous or unmapped items record a `ConversionDecision` audit entry (`DECISION_REASON_CODES`: `ambiguous_role`, `coil_without_contacts`, `contact_without_coil`, `imported_low_confidence`, `layout_candidate`, `multifunction_block`, `multipage_consolidation`, `note_reworded`, `off_grid_relayout`, `tag_collision`, `unmapped_pin`, `unmapped_symbol`) and surface a `"Conversion: needs review"` row in the Problems dock until resolved with `Accept`, `Change`, or `Keep original`.
- **Optional AI conversion advisor** — `panelcad.convert.ai_assist` adds a pluggable advisor (`mode="ai_assisted"`) for ambiguous role resolution, catalog-validated symbol counterpart suggestions, IEC-to-NEMA note rewording with one-click revert (`original_text`), and layout candidate ranking gated by hard geometry flags (`HARD_LAYOUT_FLAGS`). Every AI suggestion still passes through the deterministic From-To gate.
- **IEC PDF and scan import to NEMA** — `panelcad.convert.import_convert` runs a three-stage pipeline (`import_iec_print_to_from_to` -> `apply_from_to_corrections` -> `convert_imported_iec_print`) using `pypdf` and `panelcad.importer.vision` with no outside OCR dependencies. Low-confidence vision reads stop for From-To review and edits before NEMA conversion runs.
- **Convert and Compare dialogs** — `Tools → Convert drawing standard…` (`ConvertDialog`) adds `"Standard (rules only)"` (`rules_only`) vs `"AI-assisted"` (`ai_assisted`) mode selection (`convert-mode-combo`), `"Sequential"` (`sequential`) vs `"Keep numbers"` (`keep_numbers`) tag numbering (`convert-tag-mode-combo`, synced with `"Keep existing numbers"` `keep-existing-numbers`), `"NEMA line-reference"` (`line_ref`) vs `"Keep existing wire numbers"` (`keep`) wire numbering (`convert-wire-mode-combo`), and `Import IEC PDF / scan...`. `CompareDialog` adds a summary bar (`compare-summary-bar`), `"Highlight changed items"` overlay (`compare-highlight-toggle`), and `"Sheet Compare"`, `"From-To Diff"` (`compare-from-to-table`), and `"Decisions Review"` (`compare-decisions-table`) tabs (`Accept`, `Change`, `Keep original`).
- **18-print IEC conversion scorecard** — `panelcad.convert.scorecard` provides an 18-print synthetic and public IEC corpus (`CORPUS_IDS`: `dol_starter`, `reversing_starter`, `star_delta_starter`, `two_speed_dahlander`, `vfd_drive_cell`, `soft_starter_bypass`, `plc_digital_io`, `plc_analog_loop`, `safety_relay_dual_channel`, `control_transformer_dist`, `multi_page_conveyor_3p`, `multi_page_pump_duplex`, `multi_page_hvac_ahu`, `siemens_style_numbering`, `schneider_style_numbering`, `eplan_potential_chains`, `ambiguous_roles_sheet`, `imported_scan_fixture`) with golden NEMA From-To CSV baselines (`verify_golden_nema_from_to_csvs` against `tests/golden/wire_list`) and side-by-side `rules_only` vs `ai_assisted` comparison (`compare_scorecard_modes`).

## 0.5.9 — 2026-10-20

- **Device drag** — While a device moves, each connected wire is a straight line between its current ends. The wires are routed once when the pointer is released, and when the grab is lost. Stored waypoints on those wires are cleared, so the route ends on the pin.
- **Wire picking** — On a shared run, equal distances select the wire on top. The hit area is the stroke pad and the label, so a point inside that pad is kept. A conductor whose hit area misses the pointer is not measured. Finding the nearest pin on a print still returns the same distance.
- **Sheet routes** — Switching back to a sheet that has not changed reuses its routes and label seats. The first visit still computes them. An edit, undo, redo, a print import, or a library save drops the cache. A change on another sheet, or a change to a library symbol used on the drawing, is not served from that cache.
- **Print import** — Scoring a symbol origin stops once that origin cannot beat the score already kept. An origin with no stroke in range still scores zero. The origin that wins is the same.
- **Schematic sheets** — A power drawing that already places single-pole fuses between multi-pole devices is left as drawn. Cleanup does not gather that fuse row into one pile, so captions, tags, and phase wires stay off the symbols. A two-pin fuse follows its nearest device, and each phase lead beside a drive keeps its own lane.
- **Schematic sheets** — An upright power sheet stacks each one-to-one chain top to bottom, with phase pins in line. A device that feeds two or more branches keeps its own column. Each phase crosses to its branch on its own lane beside the feeder, so the phases are not tied together and the fuses are not bypassed. Columns scale down to 0.62 to fit between the rails, and a sheet that still does not fit says so and leaves the extra branches where they were. Branches that meet again downstream stay one chain. A tag that has to move stays on the side that faces its symbol. Terminal strips sit in their own column. The transformer and its fuses sit beside the device that feeds them. A sheet drawn with sideways starters is not restacked, and each phase of a shared bus keeps its own lane. A control sheet keeps the rail names its wires already use, and Cleanup does not rewire a ladder that already reaches both rails. Tags, wire numbers, and cross-references that would land on each other, on a symbol, or on a bus lane move to a clear seat, and a wire number stays on its own wire even when every clear seat is taken. Running Cleanup again does not move a sheet that already fits.
- **Print import** — When a motor and a contact share a tag, the contact a traced wire already names stays the placed part. Those wires stay on the contact.
- **Print import** — A conductor ends on the symbol its ink meets. A pin that only reaches into the next symbol does not take that end. A pin just outside the snap does not block the symbol the ink meets. When the pin's own tag is the nearer mark, that pin keeps the end. A coil and a contact of one name both stay, and the taller tag stays the placed part. A low-confidence box of a tag that also has a coil still occupies the rung, so a wire number cannot jump across it. Rung spacing follows the placed symbols, so a second copy of a tag does not split the row. A tag-only mark takes the clearest reading of that tag. A printed IEC aspect mark is stored as printed (`-K1` stays `-K1`).
- **App icon** — The window, the taskbar, and the desktop and Start menu shortcuts from `Install-PanelCAD.cmd` use the PanelCAD icon (enclosure, DIN rail, and wire).
- **Startup** — A splash screen shows the icon, PanelCAD, the version, and a status line (`Loading parts library...`, `Opening project...`) before the main window. The main window stays off screen until it has painted, in light and dark mode. CLI commands and offscreen tests do not show the splash.
- **Print import** — A wire number stays on the conductor it sits on. If two conductors are equally close, or the run does not reach a symbol, the number is needs-info and is not connected. An end lands on the symbol it reaches. A circle stays with the tag beside it. A second wire does not reuse a pin the closer run already has. A number nudged off one stub, still clearly nearer that stub than the next run, stays on it. A single digit at a pin is the terminal mark, not a locked wire number. Tags at nearby point sizes stay when more than one of them is printed. T1/U1/V1/W1 drawn on a conductor stay terminal marks, including a row of them that a prefix match turned into symbols. A matched device with that tag and no sibling lead (a drive named U1) stays. A model name printed on a symbol is not a second device. W101 and 101A are wire numbers; when they are not connected they are wire questions, not device rows. A line-reference column, including a skipped rung and single digits outside the rail, and rail names such as L1, L2, and 0V, are not wire questions.
- **Print import** — Wires are read from a print PanelCAD did not draw. A long orthogonal run is a conductor whatever its colour and width; colour and stroke width stay a hint. DXF LINE, LWPOLYLINE, POLYLINE, ARC, CIRCLE, and exploded INSERT blocks, and SVG lines, use that same stroke model. A run that ends on an unmatched symbol lands on a provisional side pin and is marked for review. A wire number that was read and not connected stays a Problems row (number, page position, connect by hand). Device tags use the dominant text height, so a taller rail caption does not drop them, and a wire number the same height as the tags is kept. Single-digit, 5-digit, and lettered numbers (W101, 101A, 2-15) are wire numbers; W101 and 101A count when they sit on a conductor. IEC tags with an aspect mark (-K1, -X2-4, =A1-K1) are the product designation. A title block keeps a multi-dash drawing number (LS-2041-E1), a REV on the same line, and a company name with no COMPANY label.

## 0.5.8 — 2026-10-19

- **Undo** — Each drawing edit is one undo step. A lost mouse release, or Ctrl+Z while the pointer is still down, closes that step instead of leaving the gesture open. Undo cancels the in-progress drag first, so the later release cannot push it back onto the stack.
- **Bug reports** — `panelcad bug-report` with an empty description exits 1 and prints `description is required`. It does not print a traceback.
- **Wire picking** — A wire's click target is at least 3 mm and at least about 7 screen pixels, whichever is larger, so fit-to-page is not stricter than before and a zoomed-out hairline is still easy to hit. The sheet border, the grid, and a rung symbol no longer take the click. When several wires are in that band, the closest one is selected, and it highlights under the cursor before the click.
- **Dock handles** — The library and the bottom panel can be dragged. A saved window layout keeps those sizes and does not pin the splitters. The grab bar stays usable at 100%, 125%, and 150% scaling.
- **Off-grid parts** — A plain click on a device does not snap off-grid parts onto the drawing grid or onto a rail, and it does not add an undo step. Grid and rail snap apply only to the symbols that were dragged.

## 0.5.7 — 2026-10-19

- **Needs-info marks** — An INFO NEEDED mark stays off the counted bill and the quote. The built-in symbol, any `*.needs_info` symbol, a device named INFO NEEDED, and a part whose description is exactly INFO NEEDED are listed under Needs info (not counted), with `counted` no and flag `needs-info`. The print shows that flag. The mark has no millimetres: the generic 18×80×70 table is not a size. A `bom: no` flag still means off the counted bill when YAML loads it as false.
- **Network layout** — Auto-layout no longer assigns 192.168.1.x to a device with no known IP. A stored address is kept. A blank address stays blank, and the network node shows TBD.
- **From-to wire list** — A conductor with no gauge shows `TBD`. The report does not fill a default size. A gauge on the wire, or a size on that net, on `control` / `power`, or on `*`, is still used. Color and type keep their report defaults. The stored gauge stays blank.
- **Guidance** — Import print's Advanced section starts collapsed, including the progress line. The I/O wizard keeps CSV field codes, and its module list uses words instead of library ids. Help uses Help → Updates… and the AI tab path. Problems check labels cover print-import rows and the reconstruct rows.
- **Guidance** — Help agrees that parts with no size yet stay off the BOM and the quote. The layout command is named Build layout from schematic in the menus, the empty layout, and Help. Menu commands whose tooltip only repeated the label now say what the command does. Import print keeps Workers, num_ctx, num_predict, and Ollama thinking under Advanced (AI settings). Updates… is on the Help menu, next to Check for updates.
- **Device tags that match a rail** — A wire end with a pin is that device's terminal. `L1.X2` on a line reactor is not the L1 rail. A bare `L1` or `L2` on a power or freeform sheet still draws to the rail side and still exports: SVG, PDF, the canvas, numbering, from-to, and Problems do not stop on that saved wire.
- **Print import** — A branch joins a run only at a perpendicular T. Two conductors that already end on different terminals stay apart, so a stop rung is not tied to the start rung. Three parallel leads through a breaker, contactor, or motor select the power-pole symbol in that family. A lead counts only inside that symbol's terminal span, and every added pin has to land on one. The next breaker, or the rung above, is not another pole. The other tags on a page are read once, with the catalog already loaded.
- **Print import** — Pole count follows parallel conductors, not a rung branch. A T-junction, a seal-in, or a parallel branch at one terminal is one conductor. A coil, pushbutton, pilot, limit, or contact keeps the symbol that was drawn. A rematch stays in the sheet's standard and that device's family. A breaker, contactor, overload, or motor with fewer terminals than the parallel run takes the larger variant; when none fits, that side stays needs-info and the symbol stays.
- **Print import** — Conductors that share a device take one pin each, in the order the pins are drawn. A pin that is not clearly the nearest stays needs-info. A blank imported number stays blank and unlocked, so Number wires can fill it. Symbol ink is scored from the strokes next to the candidate. An SVG line uses those same conductor widths.
- **Print import** — NO or NC is the contact blade, not the whole-symbol score: a drawn slash is NC and a missing slash is NO. IEC colon tags (`K1:14`, `-K1:14`) are read; `X1:5` stays a terminal. A line-reference column has to sit outside the rail and increase down the page, so repeated stub numbers stay wire labels. A printed wire number is not replaced by an auto number. An unknown prefix (`XT1-4`, `SOL1-2`) stays one device unless that parent is also on the sheet.
- **Print import** — An unnumbered conductor is still a wire when it lands on two terminals. A ladder line-reference column is not a wire number, on a PDF or a DXF. NO or NC comes from the contact ink; the tag suffix only breaks a tie, and a close call stays needs-info. DXF lineweight follows the layer (BYLAYER) and the INSERT (BYBLOCK), and exporter layer names are not required. Contact tags accept any aux family and an IEC colon (`TR1-13`, `KA1-14`, `OL1-95`, `K1:14`). A 3-phase L1/L2/L3 sheet does not become a control return. Drop length is a fraction of the run it meets. Equal-width pages still get conductors from the geometry outside the symbols.
- **Problems, save, and small screens** — Problems → Fix opens one dialog for the selected row (catalog, envelope, photo, connection, or nameplate). Save As into a folder you cannot write shows a permission message and keeps the drawing. A damaged or missing project file explains that in plain language, and a file you cannot read says so. A CSV title line imports as a parts list without a second "unrecognized" message. Choose another file opens the import dialog. A blank project asks once to fill in the nameplate instead of nine warnings while Problems says ready. Below 1280 px the sheet stays usable, the bottom docks scroll, and Help sits beside the sheet. Library buttons stay fully visible in that 200 px dock, and a wide window gives the Library its usual width. A Library, Inspector, or AI dock closed by hand stays closed when the window widens. Help search matches whole words, so lock does not match blocking or lockout.
- **Layout, revisions, and sheets** — Build layout from schematic keeps a sized enclosure and does not invent a generic box. The automatic page outline is not a size: layout and the thermal page report needs-info and place nothing until Size Enclosure, apply enclosure, or an explicit width and height. An old invented name (`Enclosure 600 × 300 × 150 mm, N BOM items in cabinet`) with no catalog or maker is the same. Parts that do not fit that box, including a part on a wire duct or a DIN module that is not on a rail, stay off the sheet and are listed as unplaced. Build layout names them in a dialog and on the status line, `panelcad layout` prints each reason, and the Problems row says the part does not fit that enclosure. After the enclosure is resized, that row says to build the layout again. Build layout drops rails with no DIN parts on them, and a rail does not run into the side duct. The pack does not add rails below the box. That pack does not land one part on another, including terminal end stops on a fuse, and it does not drop a needs-info part or move a DIN module off its rail. Issue revision offers the next letter after the highest revision already on the drawing and appends it. The print-package cover uses a schematic description, not an I/O continuation line. A terminal-strip sheet is on the drawing index under its strip name. Only the symbol the user just placed or moved is lifted off the title block, in that edit, and the lifted position snaps down onto the 2.5 mm grid. Any other symbol on the title block stays put and is a Problems row. Sheets can be reordered from the sheet bar, Session, and JSONL. The AI dock page line and the Checks count follow the open sheet and the Problems list.
- **Ladder rails** — A rail click starts a wire only with the Wire tool. Wiring the hot rail straight to the return is refused. A device pin such as L1.X2 is not a rail, and a sheet that does not draw rails does not refuse L1 to L2. Moving a device keeps the clicked landing. The rails are drawn down to the last numbered rung.
- **Ladder rails** — The Wire tool treats the ladder rails as terminals. Click anywhere on the left or right rail (L1 and L2, or the names in the title block) or on an IEC column's top and bottom rails (L+ and 0V, or the names you set) to start or end a wire. The junction is drawn where you clicked. The connection is saved with the project, numbered with the other wires on that rail, and included in the control power budget. Undo and redo include it.
- **Symbols and import** — A guessed part that contradicts the print falls back to the generic symbol of the same family, with pins. The pinless unknown box is only for a tag with no family. A ground terminal is not used for a tag that is not PE. Ordinary two-pin terminals share one wire number; `panelcad wire-numbering --split-feedthrough` renumbers the unlocked ones. NEMA symbols for a feed-through terminal, float, level, pressure, temperature, and flow switches, a foot switch, an off-delay timer, timer contacts (NOTC, NCTO, NOTO, NCTC), a horn, and an alternating relay are schematic only, with no millimetres. A feed-through terminal keeps one wire number on both sides; PE, disconnect, and fuse terminals stay split, and a shop can split feed-through numbers. Renaming a rail renames the wires on that rail in the same undo. A failed PDF export leaves no partial file. `panelcad wire --number` locks the number. A vendor CSV with a title line above the header imports, and a bad file prints the error without a traceback. After a print import, the summary says how many devices were placed, how many wire numbers were read, how many wires connected, and how many numbers were left unconnected, and it points at Problems when almost none connected.
